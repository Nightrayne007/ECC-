# Safety — If You Find a Package, Bale, or Buoy

Traffickers move narcotics across the Pacific in **waterproof bales**, lashed
with **rope or fishing netting**, floated on **solar-powered GPS buoys** (round
silver or yellow discs). When ropes break or a pickup fails, these wash up on
reefs, beaches, and fishing grounds.

If you see one near your island, on the reef, or in your fishing ground:

## Do

- 🚫 **Do NOT touch, open, or move it.** It may be tracked, and its contents are
  dangerous and illegal to handle.
- 📍 **Note the location** — landmarks, the reef or beach name, and **GPS
  coordinates** if you have them.
- 📸 **Photograph it from a safe distance**, if you can do so safely.
- 📞 **Report it** to your local police / authorities, and log it in KAILA's
  **Sighting Reports** module so it can be geolocated and analysed.
- 👥 **Keep others away**, especially children.

## Do not

- ❌ Do not attempt to recover, hide, sell, or dispose of it.
- ❌ Do not approach a **buoy or tracker** — the people who dropped it may return.
- ❌ Do not post exact locations publicly on social media before authorities are
  informed; that can tip off the people who are looking for it.

## Why your report matters

A single villager's report — a location, a time, a photo — becomes intelligence.
Run through drift modelling and vessel correlation, one sighting can reveal where
a drop came from and where more packages may come ashore. **Your eyes on the
water + technology watching the ocean = a coast that is not a soft target.**

Our ocean is our life — our fishing grounds, our qoliqoli, our children's
future. **See something. Say something.** Border security is everyone's
responsibility.
